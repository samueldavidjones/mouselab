Information sheet

You are invited to take part in a research study. Please read the description carefully. If you are happy to participate, click the 'Continue' button at the bottom of the page.

What is the purpose of this study?

This study will help us understand how people search for information and make decisions under uncertainty, and how this relates to everyday experiences of anxiety, worry and mood. Improving our understanding of these processes could have implications for education, health, and everyday decision-making.

What is involved in the study?

To start, we will explain a decision-making task and ask a few comprehension questions so that we can be sure you understand the task before you begin. You will then complete four blocks of the task. After some blocks we will ask a few short questions about what you learned. Finally, you will complete two short questionnaires about how you generally feel (including anxiety, worry and mood), and some optional demographic questions. The whole study should take approximately 25 minutes.

Are there any risks or disadvantages?

The questionnaires ask about things such as anxiety, worry, low mood and physical feelings of anxiety (for example, a racing heart), which some people may find uncomfortable. You can stop at any time. The questionnaires are not a diagnostic assessment, and because your answers are anonymised we cannot give you feedback on them or contact you about them. Details of organisations that offer free, confidential support are provided at the end of the study.

What will I receive for taking part?

You will receive course credit through SONA for taking part. In addition, each point you earn buys you a raffle ticket that enters you into a draw for a £25 Amazon voucher. Please try to earn as many points as you can in the task.

What if I don't want to take part?

Your participation is completely voluntary, so it is up to you whether you would like to take part. You can withdraw from the study at any point without having to provide any explanation. After the study is complete, you can withdraw your data for up to four weeks by contacting the research team (contact details below). After four weeks, data will be fully anonymised and it will no longer be possible to identify your individual data.

Will my taking part in the study be kept confidential?

Yes, your responses and data will be treated as strictly confidential. Giving basic demographic information (e.g., age, first language) at the end of the study is optional, and we request only your participant ID and digital initials as part of the consent process. Your IP address is not recorded in the dataset used for analysis.

What will happen to my data?

The Data Protection Act 2018 and UK General Data Protection Regulations allow you control over your personal data and how it is used. Under GDPR, Bangor University is the data controller in relation to your personal data and will process your data for the purposes of this study. Your answers about mood and anxiety count as health-related information and will be handled with particular care. Digital research data will be stored on a password-protected computer. At the end of the study, the anonymised dataset may be deposited on a publicly available data repository so that other scientists can examine our results.

Who is running the study?

This study is led by Dr Sam Jones of Bangor University's Department of Psychology. The study has been given a favourable opinion by a Bangor University Research Ethics Committee.

Who do I contact if I have concerns?

If you have a concern about any aspect of this study, please contact Dr Sam Jones via email at samuel.jones@bangor.ac.uk. Alternatively, you might contact Bangor University's Senior Research Governance & Policy Officer directly at seniorresearchgovofficer@bangor.ac.uk.

Task instructions

You will see a branching network with a starting location (blue circle) and 16 other locations for you to search.

Each location hides a reward value. To reveal a value, click on it.

You can only click locations next to already-revealed locations. These are highlighted in orange.

When you are ready, press the 'Execute plan' button and then use your keyboard's arrow keys to move from the start along the arrows. You can only move in one direction (you can't go back on yourself).

You can move onto any location, whether or not you have revealed it. You collect the reward at every location you move onto — including hidden ones, whose rewards may be positive or negative. Revealing a location simply lets you see its reward before you decide where to go. You can press 'Execute plan' at any time, even before revealing anything.

You don't have to navigate to the end of a path. Just click the 'Stop here' button (or press the spacebar) when navigating if you would like to stop at your current location.

Your score equals the sum of the rewards at every location you moved onto, whether or not you revealed them. You do not need to search every location.

Try to maximise your overall score.

Understanding agentic behaviour is at the frontier of cognitive neuroscience. This study investigated how people search for information and make decisions under uncertainty — for example, how much they check before committing, and how this changes when information is free or costly — and whether these patterns relate to everyday experiences of anxiety (including its physical and cognitive aspects) and low mood. The questionnaires you completed are widely used research measures; they are not a diagnosis.
